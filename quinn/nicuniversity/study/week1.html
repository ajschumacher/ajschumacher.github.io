✅ The big ideas, by course

🎓 Freshman Seminar — reread

  • Notes are for future-me: 3 big ideas, in my own words.
  • Quiz strategy: read twice, read all choices, cross out wrong ones, then choose.
⚗️ Chemistry 101 — reread
  • Everything is made of atoms. About 118 kinds (elements); living things use mostly C, H, O, N.
  • A formula is a molecule's recipe. The subscript counts the atom just before it. No number = 1.
  • A breath is a trade across the alveoli: O₂ in to the blood, CO₂ out. Too much CO₂ makes blood acidic.
🔬 Biology 101 — reread
  • A cell is the smallest living thing. Ladder: atoms → molecules → cells → tissues → organs → systems → organism.
  • Mitochondria are the cell's power plants: sugar + oxygen → energy, with CO₂ and water as exhaust.
  • Cells divide by doubling (1 → 2 → 4 → 8…). Growth costs fuel — that's why preemies need so much.
🧲 Physics 101 — reread
  • Pressure = push ÷ area. Same push on a smaller area = bigger pressure.
  • Air has weight: at sea level it holds up 760 mm of mercury — so pressure (and blood pressure) is measured in mmHg.
  • Breathing machines use gentle pressures in cmH₂O. 1 mmHg ≈ 1.36 cmH₂O because mercury is 13.6× heavier than water.
📈 Calculus 101 — reread
  • Calculus is the math of change. Big question 1: how fast is it changing?
  • Rate of change = change ÷ time. (970 − 900) ÷ 7 days = 10 grams per day. “Per” means “divided by.”
  • On a graph, steepness = rate. Steep = fast, flat = none, downhill = losing (negative). Read the line, not one dot.
📊 Statistics 101 — reread
  • Mean = add them all, divide by how many. Range = biggest − smallest.
  • Apgar score (1952): 5 signs × 0–2 points = 0–10 at 1 and 5 minutes. 7–10 good; 4–6 may need help; 0–3 needs help now.
  • Numbers can be compared and studied in thousands; “looks fine” can't. But the mean hides the spread — ask for the range.
🧬 Genetics 101 — reread
  • DNA = the instruction book in 4 letters (A, T, C, G), 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs — one of each pair from each parent. A gene = one recipe for one protein.
  • Cells read DNA 3 letters at a time (4 × 4 × 4 = 64 words) to build proteins. Every cell has the whole book; each type reads different chapters.
  • Identical twins = one egg split (same DNA). Fraternal = two eggs. Twins are usually born early and small — so they fill NICU beds.
🧠 Psychology 101 — reread
  • Babies can't talk, so psychologists measure what they DO: looking time, sucking, heart rate.
  • Newborns already hear (from ~24 weeks), recognize mom's voice, prefer faces, and see best at ~20–30 cm.
  • A newborn brain wires at over a million connections a second, shaped by experience. Preemies wire in the NICU — so: dim, quiet, protected sleep, parents close.
🏥 Sociology 101 — reread
  • Sociology = people in groups: roles, hierarchy, rules, rituals. A NICU is a small society.
  • The NICU team: doctors, nurses (most bedside hours), respiratory therapists, pharmacists, therapists, social workers, cleaners, parents. Nobody does it alone.
  • Levels I–IV; regionalization gets the sickest babies to the right level. Rounds are a ritual that makes the team reliable. Good teams let anyone speak up.
