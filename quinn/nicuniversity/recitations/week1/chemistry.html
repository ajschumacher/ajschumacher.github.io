Homework 1: Atoms & Air

Counting atoms, drawing molecules, and thinking in percent — about 20 minutes on paper. This is where yesterday's ideas turn into skills you can actually use.

How recitation works

  1. Print the homework below and have your Chemistry Class Notes sheet next to you.
  2. Do the work on paper. Part A is six rows of atom-counting — go slowly, and check each row by adding up the plain letters as 1 and the little numbers as themselves. Stuck? The Chemistry lecture is one click away.
  3. Your professor grades it with the answer key (partial credit is built in).
  4. Record the score and earn the Chemistry homework stamp 🧪.

🧪 Bonus experiment while you're at it

Breathe out slowly onto a cold mirror or window. The fog is H₂O — water your lungs breathed out. Wipe it, wait, and it's gone: the water molecules escaped back into the air. Now you've seen a molecule from the homework with your own eyes (well, a few trillion of them).
